📈 Stock Market Gap Theory

Complete Guide to Understanding and Trading Market Gaps

What is a Gap?

A gap occurs when a stock opens at a price significantly different from its previous close. This creates a "gap" in the price chart where no trading occurs between the close and open prices.

Why Gaps Matter

  • Indicate significant changes in market sentiment
  • Often signal important news or events
  • Create trading opportunities for experienced traders
  • Can indicate trend strength or weakness
  • Frequently "fill" as price reverts to equilibrium

Gap Statistics

Research shows that approximately 80-90% of gaps eventually "fill" or close within days or weeks. However, the timing and manner of gap filling can vary significantly based on the gap type and market conditions.

Key Insight: Not all gaps are created equal. Understanding the type of gap is crucial for predicting its behavior and trading it effectively.

How Gaps Form

Gaps typically form due to:

  • Earnings Announcements: Unexpected earnings can cause significant price jumps
  • News Events: Major news releases, regulatory changes, or geopolitical events
  • Market Gaps: When markets close (weekends, holidays) and open with different sentiment
  • Analyst Upgrades/Downgrades: Major changes in analyst ratings
  • Merger & Acquisition News: M&A announcements can cause large gaps
  • Dividend Announcements: Unexpected dividend changes
  • Sector Rotation: Broad market movements affecting entire sectors

Four Types of Gaps

Understanding the four main gap types is essential for gap trading. Each type has different characteristics, causes, and trading implications.

Common Gap

Common Gap

Occurrence: Most frequent type of gap

Characteristics:

  • Small price movement
  • No significant news catalyst
  • Occurs in low-volume periods
  • Fills within days

Trading Implication: Often filled quickly; low profit potential

Breakaway Gap

Breakaway Gap

Occurrence: At the end of a consolidation pattern

Characteristics:

  • Breaks through resistance/support
  • Accompanied by high volume
  • Signals start of new trend
  • Usually doesn't fill

Trading Implication: Strong signal to enter in trend direction

Runaway Gap

Runaway Gap

Occurrence: During the middle of a strong trend

Characteristics:

  • Occurs mid-trend
  • Indicates trend acceleration
  • High volume confirmation
  • Rarely fills

Trading Implication: Confirm trend strength; continue in trend direction

Exhaustion Gap

Exhaustion Gap

Occurrence: Near the end of a strong trend

Characteristics:

  • Final push of trend
  • Often accompanied by high volume
  • Signals trend reversal coming
  • Usually fills quickly

Trading Implication: Potential reversal signal; consider exiting

Gap Trading Strategies

Different gap types require different trading approaches. Here are proven strategies for each scenario.

Strategy 1: Gap Fill Trading

This is the most common gap trading strategy. It assumes that gaps, especially common gaps, will eventually fill.

Step Action Details
1 Identify Gap Look for gaps on the daily chart, especially common gaps with low volume
2 Confirm Type Verify it's a common gap (no major news, low volume)
3 Enter Trade Trade in direction opposite to the gap (if gap up, short; if gap down, long)
4 Set Target Target is the previous close (gap fill point)
5 Stop Loss Place stop beyond the gap extreme (opposite side)

Example Trade:

Setup: Stock ABC gaps up from $100 to $101.50 on light volume with no news

Trade: Short 100 shares at $101.30

Target: $100 (gap fill)

Stop Loss: $102 (above gap high)

Result: Stock fills gap in 2 days. Profit: $130

Strategy 2: Breakaway Gap Trading

Trade in the direction of breakaway gaps as they signal the start of new trends.

Step Action Details
1 Identify Consolidation Look for tight trading range (support/resistance)
2 Spot Breakaway Gap Gap breaks through resistance/support with high volume
3 Enter Trade Buy on breakaway up, sell on breakaway down
4 Set Target Use previous consolidation width as profit target
5 Stop Loss Place stop just inside the consolidation range

Example Trade:

Setup: Stock consolidates between $95-$100 for 3 weeks

Catalyst: Positive earnings gap up to $105 on high volume

Trade: Buy 100 shares at $105.50

Target: $115 (consolidation width = $5)

Stop Loss: $100 (below resistance)

Result: Stock rallies to $118. Profit: $1,250

Strategy 3: Exhaustion Gap Trading

Trade exhaustion gaps as reversal signals. These gaps often precede trend reversals.

Step Action Details
1 Identify Strong Trend Look for extended uptrend or downtrend (5+ days)
2 Spot Exhaustion Gap Gap in trend direction with VERY high volume
3 Wait for Confirmation Wait for reversal candle (opposite color)
4 Enter Trade Trade opposite to the trend direction
5 Target & Stop Target gap fill; stop above/below gap extreme

Example Trade:

Setup: Stock in strong uptrend, up 20% in 2 weeks

Signal: Gaps up 3% on massive volume (10x normal)

Confirmation: Next day opens lower and closes red

Trade: Short 100 shares at previous close

Target: Gap fill point

Result: Stock reverses, gap fills. Profit: $300

Important: Gap trading carries higher risk than traditional trading. Always use stop losses and never risk more than 2% of your account on a single trade.

Risk Management in Gap Trading

Proper risk management is critical for gap trading success. Gaps can move quickly and violently, so strict rules are essential.

Golden Rules of Gap Trading

  • Never trade without a stop loss
  • Risk no more than 2% of account per trade
  • Confirm gap type before entering
  • Check volume to confirm gap strength
  • Avoid trading gaps during earnings season volatility
  • Use limit orders, not market orders
  • Trade only liquid stocks (high volume)
  • Keep a trading journal to track results

Position Sizing

Calculate position size based on your risk tolerance and account size:

Position Sizing Formula:

Position Size = (Account Size × Risk %) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss)

Example:

Account: $10,000 | Risk per trade: 2% ($200)

Entry: $100 | Stop Loss: $98 (2-point risk)

Position Size = $200 / $2 = 100 shares

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-trading: Not every gap is tradeable. Be selective.
  • Ignoring Volume: Low volume gaps are unreliable.
  • Chasing Gaps: Don't enter after the gap has already moved significantly.
  • No Stop Loss: Always use a stop loss, no exceptions.
  • Trading Illiquid Stocks: Stick to stocks with high daily volume.
  • Averaging Down: Don't add to losing positions.
  • Ignoring News: Always check for catalysts that caused the gap.
  • Emotional Trading: Stick to your plan, don't let emotions override logic.
Warning: Gap trading can result in rapid losses. Only trade with capital you can afford to lose. Consider paper trading first to practice without real money at risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What percentage of gaps fill?

A: Approximately 80-90% of gaps eventually fill, but the timing varies significantly. Common gaps fill within days, while breakaway gaps may never fill.

Q: Can I trade gaps on intraday charts?

A: Yes, gaps occur on all timeframes. However, daily and weekly gaps tend to be more reliable for trading. Intraday gaps are more noise.

Q: What's the best time to trade gaps?

A: The first 30-60 minutes after market open is typically best. This is when gap-fill trades are most active. Avoid trading during earnings season.

Q: How do I distinguish between gap types?

A: Look at volume, news catalysts, and position in the trend. High volume + news = breakaway. Mid-trend + high volume = runaway. End of trend + very high volume = exhaustion.

Q: Should I trade all gaps?

A: No. Trade only gaps that meet your criteria. Common gaps are easiest to trade. Avoid gaps with unclear catalysts.

Q: What's the average profit from gap trading?

A: This varies widely. Gap-fill trades typically target 1-3% profit. Breakaway trades can offer 5-10%+ but with higher risk.

Q: Can I use gap trading with options?

A: Yes, but it's more complex. Options can amplify both gains and losses. Consider using spreads to limit risk.

Q: How do I backtest gap trading strategies?

A: Use stock charting software like TradingView or TC2000. Manually review past gaps and track how they behaved. Keep detailed records.
