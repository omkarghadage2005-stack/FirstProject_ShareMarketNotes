Share Market Traps

Learn common traps that traders fall into

Bull Trap

Bull Trap

A bull trap happens when a stock looks like it is breaking out upward, tricking traders into buying. Soon after, the price drops sharply.

Example: Stock breaks ₹500 resistance → traders buy → price falls to ₹460.

Bear Trap

Bear Trap

A bear trap occurs when a stock appears to break down, causing traders to short sell. Then the price suddenly rises.

Example: Stock drops below ₹300 support → traders short → price jumps to ₹340.

Pump & Dump

Pump and Dump

Promoters or groups artificially hype a stock to increase price (pump), then sell their holdings causing the price to crash (dump).

Example: Penny stock promoted on social media → price rises 200% → insiders sell → price crashes.

How to Avoid Market Traps

  • Wait for confirmed breakout with volume
  • Use stop-loss
  • Avoid hype-based trading
  • Check fundamentals and news
